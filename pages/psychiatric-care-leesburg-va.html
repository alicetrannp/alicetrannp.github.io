in Leesburg, Virginia

Leesburg holds two lives at once: the historic county seat with its brick sidewalks and courthouse green, and a fast-growing commuter town at the end of the Greenway. Alice Tran, PMHNP-BC offers psychiatric care for the people living both.

About this practice

Small-town feel, big-region pressure.

Leesburg looks like the calmest place in Northern Virginia: a walkable historic downtown, wineries out King Street, W&OD Trail weekends. But most of Leesburg commutes east into the region’s pressure cooker or works demanding jobs closer to home at Inova Loudoun Hospital, the county government, and the schools. The distance between how peaceful life looks here and how stretched it actually feels is exactly where anxiety and depression like to live.

Alice sees familiar Leesburg patterns: commuters trading three hours a day for a house with a yard, and paying for it in sleep and patience; healthcare workers at Inova Loudoun carrying the weight of everyone else’s worst days; parents in Lansdowne and River Creek managing achievement pressure that has migrated out from the inner suburbs; and longtime residents watching their town transform around them, which is its own quiet stress.

Alice Tran is a dual board-certified psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner (PMHNP-BC, FNP-BC) treating adults ages 18 to 65 for anxiety, depression, ADHD, OCD, PTSD, bipolar disorder, insomnia, and burnout. She trained under a psychiatrist with more than fifty years of experience, and she provides evaluations, medication management, and supportive psychotherapy in English and Vietnamese.

Telehealth makes the geography easy: secure video appointments reach any Leesburg living room, and no one has to explain a midday absence or drive to Fairfax after work. For those who want an in-person visit, the office at 3060 Williams Drive in Fairfax is a straight run down Route 7. Most major insurance is accepted, and new patients are usually seen within one to two weeks.

Your first appointment

Your first appointment, step by step

The first visit is a comprehensive psychiatric evaluation lasting about an hour. Expect real questions about your daily life, sleep, focus, mood patterns, medical history, and what you have already tried, asked conversationally, without judgment, and at your pace. You do not need your story rehearsed; honest and messy beats polished.

By the end, you will have an initial diagnosis explained in words you can repeat at home, and a plan you understand and agreed to. Prescriptions, when part of the plan, go to your Leesburg pharmacy electronically the same day, and your first follow-up lands two to four weeks out so the plan gets tuned on evidence, not guesswork.

Medication management

How medication management works here

Good medication management is a relationship, not a transaction. Alice prescribes and manages the full range of psychiatric medications: antidepressants, anti-anxiety options, stimulant and non-stimulant ADHD medications, mood stabilizers, and sleep aids, selected for your symptoms, health history, and preferences, then adjusted through genuine follow-up.

You will always know why a medication was chosen, what to expect in the first weeks, and which side effects to report. Partial results are treated as a signal to optimize, not a destination; too many people settle for half-better because nobody ever asked again.

Supportive psychotherapy

Supportive psychotherapy as part of care

Every appointment includes room for supportive psychotherapy: working through the stress, thought patterns, and life circumstances driving your symptoms, not just tracking a symptom score. For many Leesburg adults, this integrated approach is the right amount of support without adding a second weekly commitment.

When dedicated therapy such as CBT or trauma-focused work would help, Alice refers to trusted therapists, including options in Loudoun, and coordinates so your medication and therapy work as one plan.

Quick answers

Questions from Leesburg patients

Is there an office in Leesburg?

Appointments for Leesburg residents are by secure telehealth, which fits most schedules better than a Route 7 round trip. The in-person office is in Fairfax at 3060 Williams Drive, with free parking, for anyone who prefers it.

Can I be evaluated for ADHD without driving to Fairfax?

Yes. The full two to three visit ADHD evaluation is done by video, using the same structured process as in-person care, with prescriptions sent to your local pharmacy when appropriate.

I work nights at the hospital. Can appointments fit that?

Office hours are Monday through Thursday, 9 to 4, but 30-minute video follow-ups can usually be placed around shift patterns. Tell the scheduler your constraints and they will work with you.

Do you take Anthem and other major insurance?

Yes: Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, and Medicare among others, with coverage verified before your first visit. Self-pay rates are posted transparently on the rates page.

How long is the wait for a first appointment?

Typically one to two weeks. Loudoun County psychiatric waitlists often run months, which is exactly why this practice is built on telehealth.

What if I need therapy too, not just medication?

Supportive therapy is part of appointments here, and when structured weekly therapy would add value, you get a real referral and coordinated care rather than a name scribbled on paper.

Can you treat my college student when they are home for the summer?

If they are 18 or older and located in Virginia at the time of their appointments, yes, and telehealth means their care continues when they head back to school anywhere in the state.

Is everything confidential?

Fully. Records are HIPAA-protected, telehealth removes waiting-room encounters entirely, and nothing is shared with employers or family without your written permission.

Psychiatrist or PMHNP?

Looking for a psychiatrist in Leesburg?

Many people searching for a psychiatrist in Leesburg end up here, so it is worth being clear about who you would be seeing. Alice Tran is a board certified Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner (PMHNP-BC), not a psychiatrist. For outpatient care in Virginia that distinction matters less than most people expect: she evaluates and diagnoses mental health conditions, prescribes and manages psychiatric medication including ADHD medication, and provides supportive therapy.

The practical differences tend to favor the patient. Psychiatrist offices in this area commonly quote waits of one to three months and many do not take insurance. This practice usually sees new patients within one to two weeks, accepts most major plans including Medicare and Medicaid, and gives every new patient a full 60 minute first appointment. Alice also trained under psychiatrist Dr. Errol Segall, MD, who brings over 50 years of clinical experience.
