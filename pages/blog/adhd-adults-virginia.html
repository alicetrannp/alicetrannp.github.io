Adult ADHD self-check:
12 signs worth an evaluation

Written & medically reviewed by Alice Tran, PMHNP-BC  ·  October 2026  ·  7 min read

You have probably already taken an online ADHD quiz. Maybe three. They all said the same thing, and you still do not know whether to book an evaluation, because a quiz cannot tell you the one thing you actually want to know: is this ADHD, or is this just me?

This page is not another quiz. It is a self-check written by a prescriber who evaluates adults for ADHD every week, built around the patterns that actually predict a diagnosis, and honest about what a self-check cannot do.

The short answer

Go through the twelve signs below. Count the ones that have been true since your teens, show up at work and at home, and cost you something. Five or more, or fewer with heavy impact, is a reasonable threshold for booking an evaluation. A high count does not mean you have ADHD. A low count does not rule it out. The evaluation is what decides.

How to use this self-check

For each sign, ask three questions. Has it been there a long time? ADHD is present by adolescence, even if nobody named it. Does it show up in more than one place? Work and home, school and relationships. Does it cost you? Missed deadlines, strained relationships, money, self-respect. A sign that is recent, confined to one setting, or harmless is less likely to be ADHD.

The 12 signs

  • 1. Starting is the hardest part. You can do the task once you begin. Beginning, especially anything boring or open-ended, feels like pushing through a wall. Deadlines are what finally move you.
  • 2. Time does not feel the way other people describe it. You are chronically late or chronically early, estimate tasks badly, and are surprised by how much or how little time has passed.
  • 3. You lose things, constantly. Keys, phone, wallet, the email you were replying to, the reason you walked into the room.
  • 4. You finish about a third of what you start. Projects, hobbies, books, courses. The interest is real at the beginning and evaporates once the novelty fades.
  • 5. Your attention is either absent or absolute. You cannot focus on what you should, then lose four hours to something that caught you, and miss meals and messages while you do.
  • 6. Instructions do not stick. You nod through a conversation and retain the first sentence. Multi-step directions need to be written down or they are gone.
  • 7. Your mind is loud. Several thoughts at once, a running commentary, trouble settling even when tired. Many adults describe it as mental restlessness rather than physical.
  • 8. You interrupt, and you hate that you do. You finish people's sentences, answer before the question ends, and blurt things you wish you had held.
  • 9. Small frustrations hit hard. Irritability out of proportion, quick flashes of anger or tears, and strong reactions to criticism or feeling dismissed.
  • 10. You run on urgency. Nothing happens until it is nearly too late, and then everything happens at once. You have built your life around last-minute pressure.
  • 11. Your coping systems are extensive, and exhausting. Alarms for alarms, lists of lists, a partner who keeps the calendar. It works, barely, and it costs more effort than anyone sees.
  • 12. You have been called lazy, careless, or too much, and you have believed it. Years of trying harder without getting better leave a residue of shame that is itself a common sign of untreated ADHD.

What this self-check cannot tell you

Every one of the twelve signs can be produced by something that is not ADHD. That is the honest limitation of any checklist, and the reason an evaluation exists.

  • Anxiety wrecks concentration and makes the mind loud. Treating the anxiety sometimes resolves the "ADHD."
  • Depression drains the energy to start anything and makes finishing feel pointless.
  • Poor sleep, including sleep apnea, produces forgetfulness, irritability, and distractibility that look exactly like ADHD.
  • Thyroid problems, anemia, and some medications can mimic the whole picture, which is why lab work is often part of a first visit.
  • ADHD plus one of these. More than half of adults with ADHD also have anxiety or depression. The evaluation sorts out which is which, and which to treat first.

What happens if you book

At Alice Tran Psychiatric Care, an adult ADHD evaluation is three visits, by design, so that nothing is rushed and the diagnosis is defensible.

  • Visit 1: full psychiatric evaluation, up to 60 minutes. Your whole story, screening for anxiety, depression, and sleep, and usually lab work ordered.
  • Visit 2: the ADHD interview. Standardized rating scales, childhood and adult history, and, when possible, input from someone who knew you growing up. This visit is strictly the ADHD assessment; medication is not started here.
  • Visit 3: diagnosis and plan. Whether it is ADHD, something else, or both, and what treatment looks like: medication, skills and structure, and treatment of anything that travels with it.

The full process, including what medication options look like afterward, is laid out in Adult ADHD Treatment in Virginia: What to Expect.

The takeaway

If this page described you, you did not fail at trying harder. You may have been trying harder than almost anyone, against a brain that needed a different kind of help. An evaluation is how you find out, and it is a conversation, not a test you can fail.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I diagnose myself with ADHD using a checklist?

No. A checklist, including this one, can tell you whether an evaluation is worth your time. Only a clinical evaluation can tell you whether you have ADHD, because anxiety, depression, poor sleep, and thyroid problems can produce the same patterns.

How many signs do I need before it is worth booking?

There is no magic number. If five or more of these patterns have been present since your teens, show up in more than one area of life, and cost you something real, an evaluation is reasonable. Fewer signs with heavy impact also count.

What does an adult ADHD evaluation involve in Virginia?

At Alice Tran Psychiatric Care it takes three visits: a full psychiatric evaluation, a dedicated ADHD interview with rating scales and history, and a third visit for the diagnosis and treatment plan. Medication is not started at the ADHD interview visit.

Can ADHD be diagnosed by telehealth?

Yes. The interview, rating scales, and history work the same by secure video. Visits are available in person in Fairfax or by video anywhere in Virginia.
