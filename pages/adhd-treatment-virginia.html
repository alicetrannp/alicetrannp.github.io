ADHD care

Treatment that starts with an accurate answer.

Adult ADHD is real, common, and badly underdiagnosed, especially in women, high achievers, and adults who got good grades by working three times harder than everyone around them. It is also overdiagnosed by quiz-mill telehealth apps that hand out stimulants after one questionnaire. Both failures come from the same shortcut: skipping the evaluation. ADHD shares symptoms with anxiety, depression, sleep disorders, and thyroid problems, and a diagnosis that ignores those is a guess.

The evaluation here takes two to three visits: a complete psychiatric history first, then structured ADHD assessment using validated tools, your own self reports, and collateral input from someone who knows you well. If formal neuropsychological testing would genuinely add value, such as for suspected learning disorders, Alice says so honestly; most adults do not need it.

Treatment is built around your actual life: stimulant or non-stimulant medication when appropriate, adjusted with real follow-up rather than auto-refills; practical strategies for the executive-function side of work and home; and treatment of the anxiety or depression that decades of untreated ADHD so often produce. Appointments are in person at the Fairfax office or by secure telehealth anywhere in Virginia, in English or Tiếng Việt, with most major insurance accepted.

Before we call it ADHD

Several things imitate ADHD, and they are treated differently.

The most useful thing an evaluation does is not confirm ADHD. It is rule out the conditions that look identical from the outside, because treating the wrong one wastes months and occasionally makes things worse.

Attention, focus, and working memory are among the first things to degrade when almost anything is wrong. Chronic sleep deprivation produces distractibility, forgetfulness, and irritability that meet the surface description of ADHD exactly. So does untreated sleep apnea, which is frequently undiagnosed in adults. So does depression, where concentration problems are a core symptom rather than a coincidence. So does anxiety, where the mind is genuinely occupied elsewhere. Thyroid dysfunction, iron deficiency, perimenopause, chronic pain, and heavy alcohol or cannabis use all belong on the same list.

None of that means your attention problems are imaginary or that you are wrong about yourself. It means the same symptom has several possible causes, and the causes have different treatments. Stimulant medication does not fix sleep apnea. Sorting this out properly is the difference between treatment that works and a year spent adjusting doses of the wrong thing.

The other half of the evaluation is the part people do not expect: establishing that the pattern goes back to childhood. ADHD does not begin in adulthood. If the difficulty genuinely started at thirty, something else is going on, and that is worth finding.

Quick answers

ADHD questions from Virginia patients

How long does an ADHD evaluation take?

Plan on two to three visits. Visit one is a complete psychiatric history; the diagnosis and treatment plan come together at visit two or three. Details are on the evaluation process page.

Can stimulants be prescribed by telehealth in Virginia?

Yes, when clinically appropriate, with prescriptions sent electronically to your local Virginia pharmacy and the same monitoring standards as in-person care.

Do you take insurance for ADHD care?

Yes. Most major plans are accepted, including Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, and Medicare. See Rates for specifics.

I was treated as a teenager and stopped. Can I restart?

Yes, with a fresh look first. Adult ADHD presents differently, and the right treatment now may not be what worked at 15. Old records help; bring whatever you have.

How do I know it is ADHD and not just stress or poor sleep?

You often cannot know from the inside, which is the main reason the evaluation exists. Chronic sleep loss, sleep apnea, depression, anxiety, thyroid problems, and heavy alcohol or cannabis use all produce attention symptoms that resemble ADHD. Ruling those out is a core part of the assessment rather than an afterthought.

Does ADHD start in adulthood?

No. ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition, so the pattern is present in childhood even when nobody named it. Many adults were never assessed because they compensated well or because their presentation was inattentive rather than disruptive. If the difficulty genuinely began in adulthood, something else is likely driving it.

Do I need neuropsychological testing to be diagnosed?

Usually not. Adult ADHD is diagnosed through a thorough clinical interview and validated rating scales. Full neuropsychological testing is expensive, frequently not covered by insurance, and generally reserved for complex cases where another condition needs to be separated out.

Is an evaluation available in Vietnamese?

Yes. The full evaluation can be conducted entirely in Vietnamese with no interpreter involved, and there is a free ADHD self-screener in Vietnamese on this site.
