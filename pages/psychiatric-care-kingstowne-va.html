The short version

Quiet streets, long commutes, and care that does not add another trip

Kingstowne was planned, and it shows. Townhome clusters, sidewalks that connect, a town center where the errands live, and a rhythm that stays calm on weeknights. What the layout does not reveal is how far the people inside those homes travel each day. Franconia-Springfield Metro, the VRE platform, the run down to Fort Belvoir, the reverse commute up 495. A lot of Kingstowne households measure the week in hours behind glass.

Anh "Alice" Tran, PMHNP-BC, FNP-BC, is a dual board-certified psychiatric mental health and family nurse practitioner licensed in Virginia, seeing adults 18 to 65. For this community the case for video is simple arithmetic: a 30 minute follow-up should not cost ninety minutes of driving. In-person appointments are available at 3060 Williams Drive, Suite 300, Fairfax, VA 22031, in Merrifield, with free on-site parking.

Conditions

What gets treated

The practice covers adult psychiatry, with weight given to how a condition behaves inside a working household rather than how it looks on an assessment sheet.

Depression, including the functional kind. Depression that still makes the 6:15 train and still gets the kids fed, plus bipolar disorder and grief that has stayed past the point where anyone still asks about it. The PHQ-9 is available free, and the depression treatment page covers more.

PTSD and trauma. Trauma responses, including service-connected trauma and trauma from long before anyone put on a uniform. Symptoms that were tolerable at 30 sometimes become intolerable at 45, and that is not a failure of resolve.

Anxiety and panic. Generalized anxiety, panic that arrives in traffic, and obsessive compulsive patterns. The GAD-7 gives you a place to start, and the anxiety treatment page goes deeper.

Adult ADHD. ADHD in adults, including people diagnosed as children who stopped treatment when they enlisted or graduated and never resumed. See the adult ADHD self-check or the ADHD treatment page.

Sleep, burnout, and reproductive mental health. Insomnia built on early alarms, burnout that has crossed into clinical territory, and postpartum and perinatal mood changes.

What we hear

Reasons Kingstowne adults get in touch

A community weighted toward federal employees, contractors, and military families produces a recognizable set of stressors. None of these are unusual, and all of them are treatable.

The commute nobody counts

Two to three hours a day of travel does not appear on any symptom checklist, but it takes the same hours that sleep, exercise, and conversation would have used. People often arrive believing they are lazy when they are simply out of time.

Deployment cycles and separations

Running a household solo while a spouse is away, and then renegotiating everything when they return. Both halves of that cycle are hard, and the second one surprises people who expected relief.

Worry about the record

Hesitation to get help because of an assumption about clearances or a promotion board. Confidentiality is standard in clinical care, and treatment does not automatically jeopardize a clearance, but eligibility decisions belong to security officials, so specific questions go to a security officer or attorney.

Suburban quiet that becomes isolation

Kingstowne is peaceful, which is what people wanted. It can also mean weeks pass with no real conversation outside the household. Quiet and lonely are not the same thing, though they can look alike from the outside.

Care for parents and children at once

An aging parent in one bedroom, a teenager in another, and a full-time job in between. Caregiving that runs in two directions wears people down in a specific way, and it usually goes unnamed because it feels like ordinary duty.

Retirement from a long federal career

Thirty years of structure ends on a Friday. The loss of role, rank, and daily purpose is a genuine transition, and for some people the mood change that follows is significant rather than sentimental.

Services

What an appointment provides

Psychiatric evaluation. Sixty minutes for the first appointment, which is enough to hear a full history instead of a summary. When did this begin, what else was happening then, what have you tried and what did each attempt actually do, what runs in your family, how do you sleep, how much caffeine is in a normal day, what does an ordinary Tuesday feel like from inside your head. Patients who have been managed on one medication by a primary care office for years often discover that the original diagnosis was never examined closely. That review is part of the hour.

Medication management. Before anything is prescribed you get the reasoning: what the medication is intended to do, the realistic timeline, the side effects worth watching for early, and the plan if it does not suit you. Changes follow your response, not a template. Prescriptions go to whichever pharmacy fits your route. Controlled medications, including stimulants for ADHD, follow Virginia requirements, which can mean coming into the Fairfax office in person at some point.

Supportive therapy. Follow-ups are conversations. That can mean thinking through whether to bid on a position that adds an hour to your commute, naming what changed after a deployment, or building a sleep plan that survives a 5:00 AM alarm. Where the right answer is structured longer-term therapy, trauma processing or a full course of CBT for example, Alice will say so and help you find someone, and she coordinates with therapists her patients already see. The services page has more.

First visit

What happens the first time

You get intake paperwork ahead of time so the appointment is not spent typing. Complete what you can and leave the rest; nobody is scoring it. Your insurance is verified in advance so the cost is known before the appointment rather than after.

For a video appointment, open doxy.me/alicetran a few minutes early. There is nothing to install and no account to create. The opening minutes are conversation, and Alice usually starts with why now, because the trigger that finally produced a booking tends to be the most informative thing in the room. She works through symptoms, history, medical background, and current medications, then explains what she thinks and why. You leave with a plan you agreed to rather than one handed to you, and most patients schedule the first follow-up two to four weeks later.

Why this practice

What Kingstowne patients tend to value

The same provider every time. Not a rotating panel and not whoever happens to be free. Continuity is doing real work here, because psychiatric decisions get better as the history accumulates, and the person holding the history should be the person making the decisions.

Alice trained under psychiatrist Dr. Errol Segall, MD, whose clinical experience spans more than 50 years, and she holds dual board certification in psychiatric mental health and family practice. In a community with a lot of shift work, long commutes, and untreated sleep apnea, the family practice background matters. Thyroid function, anemia, blood pressure medications, and sleep-disordered breathing get examined rather than assumed irrelevant.

Fees are published and flat. Self-pay is $400 for the 60 minute evaluation, $200 for a 30 minute follow-up, and $250 for a 45 minute follow-up, with no memberships and no hidden fees. Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medicare, and Medicaid are accepted, and benefits are verified before you are seen. Current numbers are on the rates page, and there is more about Alice if you want to know who you would be talking to.

Appointments can be conducted fully in English or fully in Tiếng Việt without an interpreter.

Two formats

Video from home, or a drive to Merrifield

Video is the main route and the one most Kingstowne patients pick. Visits happen at doxy.me/alicetran, which opens in a browser with nothing to install, and they are always by video rather than telephone. If the connection drops you rejoin the same link, and the office calls to help you get reconnected so the appointment can be completed on video. You need to be physically in Virginia during the visit.

In-person appointments run out of the Fairfax office at 3060 Williams Drive, Suite 300, generally 25 to 35 minutes from Kingstowne, with free parking outside. Some patients do the initial evaluation in person and switch to video afterward, which is a reasonable compromise if you want to meet face to face once. Questions

What Kingstowne patients ask

I take the VRE from Franconia-Springfield and I am not home until after six. Are there later options?

Regular hours run Monday through Thursday, 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern, and evening or weekend appointments may be available on request. Many commuters solve this differently, by taking a 30 minute video visit from a quiet spot at work or from the car before the drive home, which does not require getting back to Kingstowne first.

My spouse is worried that an appointment will turn into a problem on the SF-86. Who should we actually be asking?

A security officer or an attorney who handles clearance matters. Alice is a clinician, not an adjudicator, and she does not give legal advice on clearance questions. What can be said plainly is that clinical care is confidential, that seeking mental health treatment does not automatically jeopardize a clearance, and that decisions about eligibility are made by security officials rather than by your provider. For anything specific to your situation, take it to the people who decide it.

We are a military family and may PCS next year. What happens to my treatment?

If the move keeps you in Virginia, nothing changes. If orders take you out of state, care has to transfer, because Alice can only treat patients who are physically in Virginia during the appointment. When that is on the horizon she will prepare a summary of your diagnoses, medications, doses, and what has and has not worked, so the next provider is not starting from scratch.

My teenager needs a psychiatric provider too. Can you see both of us?

No. This practice treats adults 18 to 65 only, so a teenager would need a child and adolescent specialist. Your pediatrician or your plan's provider directory is the right starting point for that. Alice can still see you, and parents often find that treating their own symptoms makes the rest of the household easier to manage.

Is there anywhere in Kingstowne to be seen face to face?

Not in Kingstowne itself. The one office is at 3060 Williams Drive, Suite 300, Fairfax, VA 22031, in the Merrifield area, with free on-site parking. From Kingstowne that is usually 25 to 35 minutes depending on traffic. Most Kingstowne patients choose video instead, which removes the drive entirely.

I work rotating shifts at a federal facility. Can my appointment times move around month to month?

Yes. Appointments are booked one at a time rather than locked into a permanent weekly slot, so each follow-up can land wherever your rotation allows. If your schedule comes out a month ahead, book at that point. If it does not, call or text (703) 791-9099 and we will find something.

Which insurance plans are in network?

Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medicare, and Medicaid. Your benefits are verified before the first appointment so you know your responsibility in advance rather than after the fact. Self-pay is also available at published rates with no membership requirement.

How long is the wait for a first appointment?

New patients are typically seen within one to two weeks, and no referral is required. If you are flexible about the day, it is often faster. You can book directly online or call or text to be scheduled.

Can prescriptions go to a pharmacy near Kingstowne Towne Center?

Yes. Prescriptions are sent electronically to whichever pharmacy is convenient for you, whether that is near the Towne Center, along Beulah Street, or somewhere on your commute. Just have the name and location handy at the appointment.

Can a follow-up be 45 minutes rather than 30 if I have a lot to get through?

Yes. Follow-ups come in 30 minute and 45 minute lengths, at $200 and $250 self-pay respectively. If you know a particular appointment will be heavy, ask for the longer one when you book. It is easier than trying to compress a complicated month into half an hour.

I cannot tell whether this is depression or just twenty years of commuting. How would you sort that out?

By looking at what happens when the commute stops. If two weeks of leave restores you, that points one direction. If you slept badly, lost interest, and felt flat through the whole vacation, that points another. Alice also checks the medical contributors that mimic depression, including thyroid function, anemia, and untreated sleep apnea, before settling on an explanation.

Psychiatrist or PMHNP?

Looking for a psychiatrist in Kingstowne?

Many people searching for a psychiatrist in Kingstowne end up here, so it is worth being clear about who you would be seeing. Alice Tran is a board certified Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner (PMHNP-BC), not a psychiatrist. For outpatient care in Virginia that distinction matters less than most people expect: she evaluates and diagnoses mental health conditions, prescribes and manages psychiatric medication including ADHD medication, and provides supportive therapy.

The practical differences tend to favor the patient. Psychiatrist offices in this area commonly quote waits of one to three months and many do not take insurance. This practice usually sees new patients within one to two weeks, accepts most major plans including Medicare and Medicaid, and gives every new patient a full 60 minute first appointment. Alice also trained under psychiatrist Dr. Errol Segall, MD, who brings over 50 years of clinical experience.
