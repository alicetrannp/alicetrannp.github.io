Orientation

A neighborhood that grew faster than its services

One Loudoun went from farmland to a full mixed-use district in not very many years. There is a cinema, a long row of restaurants, offices, apartments above the shops, and townhomes filling in behind them, with more construction usually visible from wherever you are standing. What has not kept pace is the supply of adult psychiatric care. People searching for a One Loudoun psychiatrist tend to find long waits, closed panels, or an address in Fairfax or Arlington that turns a follow-up into an evening.

Anh "Alice" Tran, PMHNP-BC, FNP-BC, is a dual board-certified psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner and family nurse practitioner licensed in Virginia, seeing adults 18 to 65. She does not have an office in Loudoun. She serves One Loudoun by video, which is the realistic way care fits into a life built around this commute.

Be clear about this first

Video is the route, and Fairfax is the office

There is no Loudoun location. The office sits at 3060 Williams Drive, Suite 300, Fairfax, VA 22031, in the Merrifield area, with free on-site parking, and it is a 40 to 60 minute drive from One Loudoun depending on the Greenway and Route 7. In-person appointments are genuinely available if you want them, and some patients do the initial evaluation face to face before switching to video. For most people here, though, the practical answer is video from the start.

Visits happen at doxy.me/alicetran, in a browser, with no app and no account. Every appointment is a video appointment; there is no telephone version. If the connection drops, you rejoin the same link and the office calls to help you get back on so the visit finishes on video. You need to be physically in Virginia during the appointment. Regional detail is on the Northern Virginia telehealth page.

Local pressures

Why One Loudoun adults call

The people who live here skew young, employed in technology or the industries that support it, and often new to the region within the last five years. Loudoun County also has a large and growing South Asian and East Asian population, and many households here are managing expectations from family across an ocean alongside a demanding job in the data center corridor. The reasons for booking cluster around a few pressures.

Always-on infrastructure work

Data center and network operations run continuously. On-call rotations, night maintenance windows, and pages that arrive at 3 AM keep the nervous system braced. Sleep debt accumulates first and mood follows a season later.

The Greenway tax

Two hours a day toward Tysons or the District, paid in tolls and attention. That time comes out of exercise, dinner, and any margin for a hard week. Commuting stress is not a soft complaint; it reshapes the whole day.

New here, and it shows

Moving for a job means arriving without a network. Neighbors are friendly and busy, weekends fill with errands, and months pass before anyone has a person to call. Isolation after relocation is one of the most common threads in first appointments.

Living inside a construction site

Detours, noise, and a skyline that changes every quarter. It sounds minor and it is not. Constant environmental unpredictability is hard on people already dealing with anxiety or sensory sensitivity.

Family expectations at distance

Sending money home, planning visits, fielding questions about marriage or grandchildren, and holding a career at the same time. Culturally aware care means these are treated as real clinical material, not background noise.

Immigration and visa uncertainty

Renewals, transfers, and timelines outside your control produce a specific, grinding kind of anxiety that does not respond to reassurance. Naming it accurately is usually the first step toward treating it.

Conditions

Concerns commonly treated

This is adult psychiatry. The focus is on how a condition affects the week you are actually living, not just which boxes it checks.

Attention. Adult ADHD, including engineers and analysts who compensated brilliantly until the job grew management responsibilities. The free adult ADHD self-check is a reasonable place to start, and the Virginia ADHD treatment page goes deeper.

Anxiety, panic, and OCD. Generalized anxiety, panic attacks that arrive on the road, and obsessive compulsive patterns that quietly take over evenings. See the GAD-7 and anxiety treatment in Virginia.

Mood. Depression, bipolar disorder, postpartum and perinatal mood changes, and grief, including grief for a parent who died overseas while you could not travel. The PHQ-9 and the depression treatment page are available.

Trauma, sleep, and burnout. PTSD and trauma responses, insomnia tied to rotating shifts, and burnout that has stopped responding to time off.

Services

What the practice provides

Psychiatric evaluation. A full 60 minutes, which is long enough to build an actual timeline instead of a snapshot. Alice asks what changed and when, what was going on around you then, what has been tried and how each attempt ended, what runs in the family, how you sleep on a work night versus a weekend, what caffeine and alcohol look like honestly, and how a typical Wednesday feels. For people newly arrived in Virginia, that history often spans other states or other countries, and it belongs in the record.

Medication management. If medication is appropriate, you hear the reasoning before a prescription exists: the expected effect, the realistic timeline, the side effects to watch in the first fortnight, and the plan if the fit is wrong. Doses change based on your response rather than a template. Prescriptions are sent electronically to a pharmacy of your choosing, including one on your route home along Route 7. Controlled medications, including ADHD stimulants, follow Virginia requirements, which can mean an in-person appointment in Fairfax at some point.

Supportive therapy. Every follow-up leaves room for conversation. That can mean working through whether to accept a role that adds travel, deciding how to talk to family about a diagnosis, or building a sleep plan that survives an on-call week. When someone needs structured, longer-term therapy such as trauma processing or a full CBT course, Alice says so and helps with the referral, and she coordinates with therapists you already see. Details are on the services page.

First visit

What happens the first time

Paperwork goes out ahead so the hour belongs to the conversation. Coverage is verified in advance, so you know your cost before the appointment rather than after. You open doxy.me/alicetran a few minutes early from wherever you are private, and the visit starts as a conversation rather than an interrogation.

Alice tends to begin with why now, since the timing of the decision usually explains something. The middle of the hour covers symptoms, history, medical background, and current medications. The end is her telling you what she thinks, in plain language, along with the options and their trade-offs. You leave with a plan you agreed to. Most people book a first follow-up two to four weeks out.

The case for this practice

What you get by choosing a small practice

The same clinician every visit. In psychiatry that continuity is not a nicety, it is the mechanism: knowing how you responded to a change three months ago is what makes the next decision straightforward instead of a guess.

Alice trained under psychiatrist Dr. Errol Segall, MD, who has more than 50 years of clinical experience, and holds dual board certification in psychiatric mental health and family practice. That second certification matters more than it sounds. Thyroid disease, iron deficiency, sleep apnea, and interactions with medications you already take are considered rather than skipped, which is often decisive when the complaint is exhaustion.

The money is published and flat: $400 for the 60 minute initial evaluation, $200 for a 30 minute follow-up, $250 for a 45 minute follow-up. No memberships, no hidden fees. Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medicare, and Medicaid are accepted and verified before the first visit. See the rates page or read about Alice.

Visits can be conducted fully in English or fully in Tiếng Việt, with no interpreter present.

Questions

What One Loudoun patients ask

Do you have an office in One Loudoun?

No, and it is worth saying plainly. The only office is at 3060 Williams Drive, Suite 300, Fairfax, VA 22031, in the Merrifield area, with free on-site parking. One Loudoun is served by video. If you want an in-person appointment you are welcome to come to Fairfax, but no one should book expecting a suite near the cinema.

I work rotating shifts supporting data center operations. Can I be seen outside the standard block?

Standard hours are Monday through Thursday, 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern. Evening and weekend times may be available on request, and they are worth asking about rather than assuming. Call or text (703) 791-9099 and describe your rotation so the office can look for something realistic.

We relocated here recently. Can you take over a medication I was already prescribed elsewhere?

Often, yes. Bring the name, the dose, how long you have been on it, and anything you remember about what you tried before. Alice reviews the history at the initial evaluation and continues what is working when it makes clinical sense. A prescription started in another state does not automatically get restarted, though, so the evaluation still happens first.

Our townhome has thin walls. Is a video visit private enough?

That is a fair concern and a common one here. Patients use headphones, a parked car in the garage, a walk-in closet, or a booked room at work. The platform at doxy.me/alicetran is secure on its side, and the part you control is the room you sit in.

How long does it actually take to drive from One Loudoun to the Fairfax office?

Roughly 40 to 60 minutes depending on the Greenway, Route 7, and the hour you pick. That drive is the main reason video is recommended for Loudoun patients. In-person visits are available for anyone who wants them, but nobody should have to spend two hours in traffic for a 30 minute follow-up.

Is Cigna accepted?

Yes. Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medicare, and Medicaid are all accepted, and your specific plan is verified before the first visit so you know the cost in advance. Self-pay is $400 for the initial 60 minute evaluation, $200 for a 30 minute follow-up, and $250 for a 45 minute follow-up.

I travel for work. What if my appointment lands on a week I am out of state?

You need to be physically located in Virginia during the visit, because that is where Alice is licensed. If a trip conflicts, move the appointment rather than joining from another state. The office can usually find a nearby slot if you call or text (703) 791-9099.

Can two people in the same household both be patients here?

Yes. Each person is evaluated and treated individually, with separate records and separate appointments, and each person's information stays confidential. This practice does not provide couples therapy or family therapy, so the care remains individual even when two people from one home are seen.

What do you need from me before the first appointment?

Intake paperwork sent ahead of time, your insurance information, a list of current medications with doses, and any past psychiatric treatment you can recall. Old records are helpful but not required. Fill in what you can and leave the rest for the conversation.

Is medication the only thing on offer?

No. The practice provides psychiatric evaluations, medication management, and supportive therapy. Follow-up appointments include real conversation about what is happening in your life. If a structured, longer-term therapy is the right fit, Alice will say so and help you find a therapist.

Does anything I say get shared with my employer or reported anywhere?

Your care is confidential and protected under HIPAA. Nothing is sent to an employer without your written authorization. Insurance claims carry limited required information, and the narrow legal exceptions involving immediate danger are the same ones that apply to any clinician in Virginia.

Psychiatrist or PMHNP?

Looking for a psychiatrist in One Loudoun?

Many people searching for a psychiatrist in One Loudoun end up here, so it is worth being clear about who you would be seeing. Alice Tran is a board certified Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner (PMHNP-BC), not a psychiatrist. For outpatient care in Virginia that distinction matters less than most people expect: she evaluates and diagnoses mental health conditions, prescribes and manages psychiatric medication including ADHD medication, and provides supportive therapy.

The practical differences tend to favor the patient. Psychiatrist offices in this area commonly quote waits of one to three months and many do not take insurance. This practice usually sees new patients within one to two weeks, accepts most major plans including Medicare and Medicaid, and gives every new patient a full 60 minute first appointment. Alice also trained under psychiatrist Dr. Errol Segall, MD, who brings over 50 years of clinical experience.
