About this practice

The healthcare desert ends at your kitchen table.

Greensville County and Emporia know exactly what a provider shortage feels like: primary care stretched thin, specialty care a highway trip, and psychiatric care functionally out of reach for anyone without a free weekday and a full tank. When access costs that much, people understandably stop seeking it, and depression, anxiety, and untreated ADHD quietly become the region’s background noise.

Alice treats Emporia patients with the same unhurried standard as anyone in Fairfax: farm and warehouse families whose work does not pause for feelings; I-95 corridor workers keeping the trucks and travelers moving; caregivers looking after parents in a county where services are scarce; and adults who long ago concluded that struggling silently was just what people here do.

Alice Tran is a dual board-certified psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner (PMHNP-BC, FNP-BC) treating adults 18 to 65 for anxiety, depression, ADHD, OCD, PTSD, bipolar disorder, insomnia, and burnout, entirely by secure telehealth. Most major insurance is accepted, including Medicaid and Medicare, and new patients are typically seen within one to two weeks, not months.

Your first appointment

What your first appointment looks like

Your first visit is a full psychiatric evaluation over secure video, about an hour: what brought you in now, your sleep, energy, mood and focus, your medical history, and what you have tried before. It is a conversation, not an interrogation, and you set the depth on anything difficult.

You leave with an initial diagnosis explained in plain language and a treatment plan you helped shape. Prescriptions go electronically to your local pharmacy the same day when medication is part of the plan, and the first follow-up lands two to four weeks later so treatment gets adjusted on real results.

Medication management

Medication management, statewide

Alice prescribes and manages the full range of psychiatric medications: antidepressants, anti-anxiety medications, stimulant and non-stimulant ADHD treatments, mood stabilizers, and sleep medications. Every choice is explained: why this one, what to expect in the first weeks, and which side effects matter.

Follow-ups run closer together while doses are adjusted and space out once you are stable. Partial improvement is treated as a signal to optimize, not a stopping point, and refill questions between visits get answered.

Supportive psychotherapy

Supportive psychotherapy included

Appointments include supportive psychotherapy: practical, evidence-informed work on the stress, thought patterns, and habits underneath the symptoms. For many patients, medication plus meaningful conversation with one clinician is exactly the right level of care.

When structured weekly therapy such as CBT or trauma-focused work would add value, Alice says so plainly and helps you find a therapist, then coordinates so both halves of treatment pull together.

Quick answers

Questions from Emporia patients

Is my connection good enough for video visits?

A phone on cellular data works fine, and if video drops, you can rejoin on the same link rather than losing the visit, and the office will call to help you reconnect so the appointment can be completed on video.

Do you take Medicaid?

Yes, along with Medicare and major commercial plans, with coverage verified before the first visit.

How fast can I actually be seen?

Typically one to two weeks, which for this part of Virginia may be the biggest difference of all.

Can you work with my doctor at the local clinic?

Yes, with your permission: your primary care stays local, Alice handles the psychiatric side, and notes travel both ways.

What conditions do you treat most from rural areas?

Depression, anxiety, and insomnia lead, with adult ADHD close behind, often all tangled together after years without care.

Is this affordable without insurance?

Self-pay rates are posted openly: $400 evaluations and $200 to $250 follow-ups, with no membership fees.

Will anyone local know?

No. No waiting rooms, no local office, records HIPAA-protected by an independent practice hours away.

What about a crisis?

Not an emergency service. Call or text 988, contact the Crater District CSB network, or go to the nearest emergency department.
