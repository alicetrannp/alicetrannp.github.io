Depression care

Depression is a medical condition, not a character flaw.

Depression does not always look like crying. For many Virginia adults it looks like numbness, irritability, a short fuse with the people they love, or getting through the workday and then having nothing left. High functioning depression is real and easily missed: the deadlines are met, the emails go out, and nobody around you knows that the inside has gone flat. Major depressive disorder comes in episodes. Persistent depressive disorder is lower in intensity and longer in duration, sometimes so long that people mistake it for their personality. A seasonal pattern is common in the mid Atlantic winter.

The first appointment is a full 60 minutes, and a real depression evaluation does more than confirm that you feel low. It asks how long, what changed, how you sleep, how you eat, what you have already tried and at what dose, and whether alcohol or a substance is part of the picture. It screens deliberately for bipolar disorder, because a history of elevated, sped up, or unusually irritable periods changes the treatment completely and an antidepressant alone can destabilize someone who actually has bipolar depression. It also considers thyroid disease, anemia, vitamin deficiency, sleep apnea, grief, and burnout, which can all imitate depression. The PHQ-9 is used to track your trajectory, not to label you.

Antidepressants help most people with clinical depression, and the honest framing is that the first one is a starting point rather than a verdict. Most people notice something at two to four weeks and see the fuller effect by six to eight. If a medication does not work or the side effects are not livable, that is expected and it is information: the plan then moves to a dose change, a switch, or an added agent, chosen from your actual response and history. Supportive therapy and psychoeducation run through every visit, and Alice coordinates with your therapist when you have one. Care is in person at the Fairfax office in the Merrifield area, with free parking, or by secure telehealth anywhere in Virginia, in English or Tiếng Việt.

Major depressive disorder

Episodes that flatten mood, sleep, energy, and interest. Treatable, and worth treating properly rather than partially.

High functioning depression

Everything looks fine from the outside. The evaluation takes what is happening inside just as seriously.

When the first antidepressant failed

One medication that did not work is not the end of the list. Dose, switch, and augmentation are all on the table.

Depression with anxiety, grief, or burnout

These overlap constantly. Sorting out which is which is the difference between a plan and a guess.

Quick answers

Depression questions from Virginia patients

How quickly can I get a depression appointment in Virginia?

New patients are typically scheduled within one to two weeks, and no referral is required. You can book directly online. Office hours are Monday through Thursday, 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern.

What happens at the first depression visit?

It is a full 60 minutes. Alice takes a complete history covering mood, sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, and function, asks what has already been tried and at what dose, screens for bipolar disorder and for medical contributors such as thyroid disease and sleep problems, and then builds a treatment plan with you rather than for you.

How long do antidepressants take to work?

Most people notice some change at two to four weeks, with the fuller effect around six to eight weeks. Sleep and energy often improve before mood does. Follow up visits exist precisely to check that progress and adjust rather than to renew a prescription automatically.

My first antidepressant did not work. Does that mean nothing will?

No. It is common for the first medication to be a partial fit, and that result is useful information. Options include adjusting the dose, switching to a different agent, or adding a second medication. Choosing among them depends on your specific response, side effects, and history.

Why do you ask about bipolar disorder if I came in for depression?

Because bipolar depression looks like depression at the visit but responds differently to treatment, and an antidepressant given alone can destabilize mood in someone who has it. Asking about past periods of unusually elevated, sped up, or irritable mood is a safety step, not a suggestion that you are being doubted.

Can depression be treated entirely by telehealth in Virginia?

Yes. Evaluation, medication management, and supportive follow up all work well over secure video. Alice is licensed in Virginia only, so you need to be physically in Virginia at the time of the visit. She treats adults 18 to 65.

What does depression treatment cost, and do you take insurance?

Aetna, Anthem, Cigna, Medicare, and Medicaid are accepted. Self pay rates are $400 for the 60 minute initial visit and $200 for a 30 minute follow up or $250 for a 45 minute follow up.

What should I do if I am having thoughts of suicide?

Please do not wait for an appointment. Call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, which is free, confidential, and available 24 hours a day. For an emergency call 911 or go to your nearest emergency room. This practice does not provide crisis or after hours coverage.
