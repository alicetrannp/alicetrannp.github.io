Federal Employee Program

Psychiatric care for the federal workforce.
In network with BCBS Federal Employee Program.

If you are a federal employee, an annuitant, or a family member carrying a Blue Cross Blue Shield FEP plan, your visits here are in network.

Who this is for

The plan most of the federal workforce carries

The Blue Cross Blue Shield Federal Employee Program, often just called FEP or the Service Benefit Plan, is offered through the Federal Employees Health Benefits Program. It is the largest carrier in FEHB, which is why so many cards in Northern Virginia carry it.

Alice Tran Psychiatric Care is enrolled with BCBS FEP for the state of Virginia. Your psychiatric visits are billed in network, at in-network rates.

That matters more here than almost anywhere in the country. The Washington region holds one of the densest concentrations of federal employees and contractors in the United States, and finding a psychiatric provider who takes the plan you already have is a recurring frustration for people who otherwise have excellent coverage.

  • ✓ Current federal employees across every agency
  • ✓ Federal annuitants and retirees who kept their FEHB coverage
  • ✓ Covered spouses and adult dependents on the same enrollment
  • ✓ Members of any FEP option, though your cost sharing differs by option
Know your option

Standard, Basic, and Focus are not the same plan

FEP is not one product. It is a family of options with meaningfully different rules, and knowing which one you carry is the single most useful thing you can do before booking.

  • ✓ Standard Option is the most flexible. It generally does not require a referral for outpatient behavioral health, and it provides some coverage out of network, though at a higher cost to you.
  • ✓ Basic Option requires you to use Preferred providers. Out-of-network care is generally not covered except in an emergency, so being in network is not a nice-to-have on this option, it is the whole thing.
  • ✓ FEP Blue Focus uses a narrower network and a different cost structure. If you carry Focus, confirm behavioral health coverage specifically before your first visit.
  • ✓ Whichever option you have, the number on the back of your card is the authority on your own copay and deductible, not this page.
Before your first visit

Four questions worth asking

You do not need a referral on most FEP options, but confirming your own numbers takes five minutes and prevents surprises.

01

Which option am I enrolled in?

Standard, Basic, or Focus. It is printed on your card, and it changes the answer to everything below.

02

What is my cost for an outpatient behavioral health visit?

Ask specifically about behavioral or mental health, since the general medical line may quote you something different.

03

Is telehealth covered at the same rate as an office visit?

Ask directly rather than assuming. It usually is, and it is worth hearing.

04

Is a PMHNP covered the same as a psychiatrist?

Psychiatric mental health nurse practitioners are covered providers, but this is a fair question to confirm for your own peace of mind.

A word on clearances

The concern that stops people from calling

A large share of FEP members in this region hold a security clearance, and the question that comes up first is almost never clinical. It is whether getting care creates a problem.

Clearance adjudication looks at judgment, reliability, and whether a condition is being managed. Seeking treatment is generally read as evidence of good judgment. Untreated problems that spill into work, finances, or relationships are what create risk. The standard security questionnaire has also narrowed over the years and now carves out care related to grief, family and marital adjustment, and adjustment from service in a combat environment.

This is not legal advice, and your facility security officer is the right person for questions about your specific agency. But if the only thing standing between you and an appointment is the assumption that treatment ends a career, that assumption is worth checking before you let it decide for you.

Separately, and regardless of clearance: your treatment record belongs to you. It is not released to an agency, a command, or an employer without your written authorization, outside the narrow legal exceptions that apply to every medical provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you take BCBS Federal Employee Program in Virginia?

Yes. Alice Tran Psychiatric Care is enrolled with the Blue Cross Blue Shield Federal Employee Program for the state of Virginia, so your psychiatric visits are billed in network.

Is FEP the same as regular Anthem Blue Cross?

They are related but distinct. FEP is the federal employee plan offered through FEHB, while Anthem is the Blue Cross company serving Virginia and administering the local network. This practice is in network with both, so either card works here.

Do I need a referral from my primary care doctor?

On most FEP options, no referral is required for outpatient behavioral health. Rules differ between Standard, Basic, and Focus, so it is worth confirming with the number on the back of your card before your first appointment.

I have Basic Option. Does that change anything?

It makes being in network essential. Basic Option generally does not cover out-of-network care except in emergencies, so seeing a Preferred provider is the difference between a copay and the full cost. This practice is in network.

Are telehealth visits covered the same as office visits?

They generally are, but confirm it for your specific option. Visits at this practice are conducted in person at the Fairfax office or by secure video anywhere in Virginia, never by telephone.

Can my spouse or adult child on my enrollment be seen?

Yes. Adults covered under your FEP enrollment can be seen, including spouses and adult dependents.

Will my agency find out I am getting care?

Not from this practice. Your treatment record is not released to an agency or an employer without your written authorization, outside the narrow legal exceptions that apply to every medical provider.

Can visits be conducted in Vietnamese?

Yes. Evaluation, medication management, and supportive therapy are all available fully in Vietnamese, with no interpreter involved.

A note on urgency
