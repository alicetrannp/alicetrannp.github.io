Online Psychiatric Care in Virginia

How to Choose an Online
Psychiatric Provider in Virginia

Video psychiatric care works well when the practice behind the screen is set up properly. Here is what to check before you book, and how this practice answers each question.

Book an Appointment →
Why it matters

The screen is not the variable. The practice is.

Research over the past decade has found telehealth psychiatric care comparable to in-person care for depression, anxiety, PTSD, and ADHD. What varies from one online practice to another is not the video connection. It is whether the provider is licensed where you live, how long the evaluation is, who you see at follow-up, and what happens when you need something a video call cannot provide. Those are the questions worth asking. For how a video visit itself works, see online psychiatry across Virginia.

Before you book

Seven things to check

  • Licensed in Virginia. The provider must hold an active Virginia license, whatever state they sit in. Verify it on the Department of Health Professions lookup before the first visit.
  • Video, not telephone. A psychiatric evaluation depends on seeing the person. Confirm the platform is video and HIPAA-compliant, and that phone-only visits are not the norm.
  • Evaluation length. Ask how long the first appointment is. A thorough evaluation takes about an hour, and some take two or three visits. Ask what happens if the picture is not clear after one.
  • Prescribing policy stated up front. Especially for ADHD stimulants and benzodiazepines. A practice that explains its rules before you book has thought about them.
  • The same clinician each visit. Continuity is part of the treatment. Ask who you will see at follow-up and how long follow-up visits are.
  • An in-person option. Some situations call for a room rather than a screen. A practice with a physical office in Virginia can offer both.
  • Published rates and insurance. Self-pay prices, the in-network list, and the cancellation policy should be on the website, not disclosed after the first visit.

Common questions

Choosing an online provider, answered

How do I check that an online psychiatric provider is licensed in Virginia?

Search the provider by name on the Virginia Department of Health Professions license lookup. A nurse practitioner will show a license from the Boards of Nursing and Medicine; a physician will show a Board of Medicine license. The license must be active in Virginia, regardless of where the provider is physically located.

Can an online provider prescribe ADHD medication in Virginia?

Yes, within federal and state rules for controlled substances. Ask before booking how the practice handles stimulant prescriptions by video, whether an in-person visit is ever required, and what its policy is on benzodiazepines. A clear answer up front is a good sign.

Is a 15-minute online evaluation enough?

Ask how long the initial evaluation is. A thorough psychiatric evaluation covers current symptoms, history, medical factors, and goals, and that takes about an hour. Some evaluations reasonably continue over a second or third visit.

What if I want to see someone in person later?

Ask whether the practice has a physical office in Virginia and whether you can switch between video and in-person visits. Alice Tran Psychiatric Care sees patients in person in Fairfax and by video statewide, and patients move between the two.
